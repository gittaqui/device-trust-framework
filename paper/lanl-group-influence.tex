\subsection{Post-hoc Dependence and Influence Sensitivity}
We audited whether the matched-pair directional comparison is driven
by a single repeated user, source host, or overlapping-window episode.
For each grouping, we removed one group at a time and recomputed the
fraction of remaining matched pairs in which the red-team target's
process count exceeded its unlabeled temporal control. The user groups
contained 1, 4, 5, and 8 matched pairs; the two source-host groups
contained 4 and 14. Thirteen episodes were formed by joining
consecutive target times separated by at most 1,200 seconds, without
claiming that these episodes represent independent attacks.

These fractions have changing denominators: leaving out the larger
source-host group retains only four matched pairs, making its 100\%
value particularly unstable. The ranges are \emph{descriptive
influence diagnostics}, not confidence intervals or evidence of
independent replication. All groups originate from the same selected
cohort; seven additional targets remain unmatched, and no temporal
control is verified benign. Standard independent-pair inference is
inappropriate with this degree of repeated-entity dependence
\cite{cameron2015cluster}. This analysis does not validate device
compliance, access-decision outcomes, or the eight-factor trust model.
