# Missing-Telemetry Results — 2026-09-04

**Synthetic experiment only. These results do not demonstrate production effectiveness.**

## Objective

Evaluate how the trust model behaves when one or more telemetry dimensions are unavailable. This matters because public enterprise datasets such as LANL do not contain all proposed endpoint dimensions, and production telemetry can also be delayed, unavailable, or partially collected.

## Strategies

- `renormalize`: ignore missing factors and renormalize the remaining weights.
- `neutral`: impute missing factors to 0.5.
- `pessimistic`: treat missing factors as zero safety contribution.
- `confidence`: calculate the observed-factor score and multiply by observed weight coverage.
- `step_up`: renormalize observed factors, but require `STEP_UP` when observed weight coverage is below 0.75 or when threat/security-coverage evidence is missing.

Two missingness models were evaluated on the same 50,000-row synthetic population:

- **MCAR:** every signal has the same independent probability of being missing.
- **Structured outage:** threat risk, security coverage, and identity assurance are 3x more likely to be missing than other dimensions. This is a stress model, not an empirically estimated outage distribution.

## Interpretation

Three findings are worth carrying forward as hypotheses:

1. **Renormalization is unsafe under missing evidence in this synthetic design.** As missingness rises, it increasingly allows unsafe sessions because absent adverse evidence is removed from the denominator rather than represented as uncertainty.
2. **Pessimistic/confidence penalties strongly suppress false allows but can create severe false-denial and friction costs at high missingness.** This makes them poor default policies without additional calibration.
3. **Explicit step-up is a promising abstention mechanism.** It avoids synthetic false denials because uncertain safe sessions are escalated rather than denied, while reducing false allows as missingness increases. The trade-off is substantial user friction: under the structured 40% outage, 96.41% of safe sessions require step-up.

These results do **not** establish an optimal missing-data strategy. The missingness distributions and safe/unsafe labels are generated by the project's synthetic scenario model. They demonstrate a methodological failure mode and motivate evaluation on independently sourced telemetry.
