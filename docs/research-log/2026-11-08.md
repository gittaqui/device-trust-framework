## Finding: conditioning on defined bootstrap replicates
The current `paired_effect` samples all N devices with replacement and then discards bootstrap replicates containing no unsafe devices. If K of N devices are unsafe, the probability of an undefined replicate is **((N-K)/N)^N**. This is an exact consequence of independent with-replacement draws, not a simulated measurement. For N=100 and K=2, this probability is 0.98^100 = approximately 0.13262, or about 265 undefined draws in 2,000 replicates in expectation. With N=100 and K=10 it is 0.9^100 = approximately 0.00002656. Thus the current percentile CI silently conditions on a nonempty unsafe bootstrap sample, and its effective replicate count depends on prevalence. Its existing `undefined_replicates` output makes this auditable, but the interval is not an unconditional ordinary percentile bootstrap CI.

## Methodological decision and planned remedy
The target estimand is the interval-weighted paired unsafe-ALLOW difference conditional on an unsafe device. When labels are fixed by synthetic scenario design, the primary inference should bootstrap **unsafe devices only**, resampling their complete paired longitudinal records, and report K explicitly. This avoids undefined denominators and matches the conditional estimand. Preserve the existing all-device bootstrap as a sensitivity analysis, with its undefined-replicate fraction reported; do not equate the two bootstrap sampling designs. Separately analyze safe DENY and STEP_UP with safe-device clusters. When estimating prevalence-weighted population burden rather than conditional unsafe risk, sample the full device population and predefine handling for zero-unsafe resamples instead of silently dropping them. If cross-device outage episodes are shared, resample episodes, not individual devices.

## Acceptance tests before replacing the implementation
1. Fixed-seed deterministic outputs and rejection of invalid counts and duplicate IDs.
2. Conditional unsafe-only bootstrap never produces undefined denominators when K>=2.
3. Constant per-device effects yield a degenerate CI at that effect within floating-point tolerance.
4. Unequal device interval counts retain the stated interval-weighted estimand.
5. In a sparse-unsafe fixture (N=100, K=2), confirm the original method can yield undefined draws, and the conditional method does not.
6. Explicitly label all CI statements as conditional on synthetic generator assumptions; compare 2,000 and 5,000 draws and conduct coverage simulations before asserting nominal 95% coverage.
