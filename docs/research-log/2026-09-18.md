## Results

Across ALLOW thresholds 0.60–0.95, ordinary-access coverage falls monotonically. The most informative synthetic transition is between 0.75 and 0.80: conditional unsafe-ALLOW risk falls from 8.71% to 0.29%, while safe-session ALLOW recall falls from 94.60% to 86.42%. At 0.85 the finite synthetic sample has zero observed unsafe ALLOWs, with safe-session ALLOW recall of 85.81%.

## Interpretation

This reframes STEP_UP as an abstention/reject action and makes the security-versus-coverage trade-off visible. It also prevents a misleading presentation in which a high threshold appears unambiguously superior merely because unsafe ALLOW decreases.

## Limitations

All outcomes are generated from authored synthetic scenarios. Zero observed unsafe ALLOW at high thresholds is not a security guarantee. The experiment does not estimate production prevalence, attack success, user friction, or the probability that STEP_UP successfully resolves uncertainty. Previous dependence and distribution-shift experiments demonstrate that favorable synthetic operating points can deteriorate when assumptions change.

## Next step

External validation remains the highest priority. If external telemetry is still unavailable, add confidence intervals/bootstrapped uncertainty around the risk–coverage curve and repeat the curve under the correlated-signal stress conditions, rather than treating one synthetic population as definitive.
