## Results

The full additive 0.75 model produced 12.50% unsafe false ALLOW and 5.40% safe STEP_UP on the current synthetic population. Removing identity assurance increased false ALLOW to 25.33% (+12.82 percentage points); removing anomaly safety increased it to 23.71% (+11.21 pp); removing threat safety increased it to 16.91% (+4.41 pp); and removing freshness increased it to 15.10% (+2.60 pp).

Removing compliance reduced false ALLOW to 1.98% and safe STEP_UP to 0%. This is not evidence that compliance is harmful in real deployments. It exposes the current generator's deliberate construction: several unsafe scenarios retain high compliance while the safe policy-drift scenario has low compliance. Renormalization then transfers compliance weight to other dimensions that better separate those synthetic scenarios.

Removing the weighted security-coverage term reduced false ALLOW to 2.34%, but the critical-security-coverage hard gate remained active. Therefore this condition does not measure the total value of security coverage.

## Scientific interpretation

The experiment improves construct transparency. It shows which conclusions are strongly dependent on particular dimensions under the synthetic design and identifies a key risk for the manuscript: signal ablation cannot be described as real-world feature importance. The largest degradations are aligned with the generator's identity-risk and adversarially-compliant scenario definitions.

The verified Yan et al. survey supports the broader motivation that explanations in cybersecurity should help operators understand decisions, validate model behavior, and diagnose errors. It does not provide evidence that this project's signal ranking is correct.

## Limitations

- All reported ablation outcomes are synthetic.
- Ablations renormalize remaining weights, so each result is a policy perturbation rather than a pure zeroing effect.
- Scenario prevalence and feature ranges were authored by this project and can induce design alignment.
- Hard gates remain active, which changes the interpretation of weighted-factor ablations.
- No production or confidential employer data were used.
- External validation remains outstanding.

## Next step

The highest-value next step remains the frozen external-validation protocol on official LANL telemetry. Before that run, the manuscript should incorporate this ablation as a construct-validity diagnostic, not as a claim of feature importance. If external telemetry access remains unavailable, the next synthetic study should vary scenario prevalence independently of feature ranges to quantify how much headline policy rankings depend on the assumed population mixture.
