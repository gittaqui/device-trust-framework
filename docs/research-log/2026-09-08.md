## Fresh literature verification

Verified bibliographic metadata for:

Thomas G. Dietterich, "Approximate Statistical Tests for Comparing Supervised Classification Learning Algorithms," *Neural Computation*, vol. 10, no. 7, pp. 1895–1923, 1998. DOI: `10.1162/089976698300017197`.

The publisher record states that the paper evaluates several approximate tests for classifier comparison and finds McNemar's test to have low Type-I error for a single fixed evaluation set. This supports using a paired McNemar comparison for deterministic policies evaluated on the same synthetic rows. The reference was added to `paper/references.bib`.

## Results

Seed: `20260903`; rows: 50,000; safe rows: 29,033; unsafe rows: 20,967.

| Policy | False ALLOW | 95% CI | Safe STEP_UP | 95% CI | Ordinary-access accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Binary compliance | 93.74% | 93.40%–94.06% | 0.00% | 0.00%–0.01% | 52.45% |
| Additive 0.75 | 12.50% | 12.06%–12.96% | 5.40% | 5.15%–5.67% | 91.62% |
| Additive 0.80 | 0.34% | 0.27%–0.43% | 13.58% | 13.19%–13.98% | 91.97% |
| Guarded 0.75 | 1.24% | 1.10%–1.40% | 5.40% | 5.15%–5.67% | 96.34% |

Selected paired McNemar comparisons:

- additive 0.75 vs additive 0.80: first-only correct = 2,375; second-only correct = 2,549; continuity-corrected chi-square = 6.078; p = 0.0137;
- additive 0.75 vs guarded 0.75: first-only correct = 0; second-only correct = 2,361; p < 1e-300 under the asymptotic one-degree-of-freedom calculation;
- additive 0.80 vs guarded 0.75: first-only correct = 260; second-only correct = 2,447; p < 1e-300.

The most important result is not the p-values. The threshold-0.80 policy produces the lowest synthetic false-ALLOW rate but materially more safe STEP_UP decisions. The guarded policy occupies a different security/friction trade-off under the current generator. Because benign synthetic identity behavior does not overlap the guard region enough, its apparent friction advantage remains vulnerable to design-overfitting.

## Limitations

- All effectiveness values are synthetic and conditional on generator assumptions.
- The same synthetic design influenced policy development; inferential statistics do not remove this design-overfitting risk.
- The binary compliance baseline is intentionally weak against scenarios designed to stay nominally compliant; its 93.74% false-ALLOW rate is not an estimate of real compliance-system performance.
- Wilson intervals quantify sampling variability under the generator, not external validity.
- McNemar tests are exploratory because the evaluated policies were iteratively developed against the same synthetic framework.
- No confidential employer data were used.
- No real-world LANL effectiveness result is claimed.
