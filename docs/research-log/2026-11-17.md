## Research question and method
How much finite-lag Bartlett tapering can underestimate variance when telemetry outage episodes are serially correlated? For stationary Gaussian AR(1) shocks, the **exact finite-sample variance inflation** of the episode mean is `1 + 2 sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-k/n)phi^k`. Compare it to a **population-covariance oracle Bartlett approximation** `1 + 2 sum_{k=1}^L(1-k/(L+1))phi^k`. This comparison isolates lag truncation and taper effects. It is NOT an actual sample Newey–West estimator: no mean estimation, sampling variability, finite-sample correction, or CI coverage is evaluated. In particular, this Bartlett oracle targets a long-run variance approximation, not the exact finite-sample mean variance.

## Executed deterministic synthetic-model results
For phi=0.9:

| Episodes | Exact mean-variance VIF | Bartlett oracle L=3 | Oracle / exact | Bartlett oracle L=8 | Oracle / exact |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 7.276212 | 3.524500 | 48.44% | 6.748410 | 92.75% |
| 30 | 13.254347 | 3.524500 | 26.59% | 6.748410 | 50.91% |
| 100 | 17.200048 | 3.524500 | 20.49% | 6.748410 | 39.23% |

These ratios demonstrate that short bandwidths may miss much of the serial-dependence variance even with **known** covariances. They do not establish actual HAC confidence-interval coverage or security effectiveness. A 36-condition deterministic grid was executed; six regression tests passed.

## Limitations and next step
No enterprise data, real endpoints, security decision measurements, or empirical confidence-interval coverage. Oracle assumes known stationary AR(1) autocovariances and equal episode weighting. No persistent device effects or cross-sectional dependence. Next: simulate the **actual sample-mean-centered** episode Student-t and Newey–West HAC confidence intervals with predeclared bandwidths and 10/30/100 episodes, measure Monte Carlo coverage and MC uncertainty, and compare against an episode-block bootstrap. Do not claim HAC calibration from this oracle audit.
