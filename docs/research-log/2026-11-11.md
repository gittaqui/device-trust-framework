## Repository state and contribution
Inspected main-branch `src/conditional_unsafe_bootstrap.py`, `experiments/conditional_bootstrap_coverage_2026_11_10.py`, and November 10 log. Preserved the existing bootstrap and scoring engine. Added an **exact Clopper–Pearson binomial interval benchmark** for the previous day's independent, two-point, **equal-observation-length** synthetic fixture. This is a restricted benchmark, NOT a replacement for the general interval-weighted paired longitudinal estimator.

## Method and results (mathematical, synthetic)
For K independent unsafe devices, each device's effect is 1 with probability p=.3 and 0 otherwise; equal observation lengths yield X~Binomial(K,.3). Invert the two binomial tails at alpha/2 to obtain exact Clopper–Pearson bounds for every X. Enumerate all X=0,...,K and sum their known binomial probabilities when the interval contains p=.3. This is deterministic coverage enumeration, not Monte Carlo.

| K | Exact Clopper–Pearson 95% coverage |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 1.000000 |
| 5 | 0.997570 |
| 20 | 0.975218 |
| 100 | 0.962549 |

Compare with November 10's simulated percentile-bootstrap fixed-length coverage: 0.4467, 0.8367, 0.9767, 0.9700, respectively. The exact interval is conservative at small K and does not establish optimality. For K=2 its coverage is 100% at p=.3 because all three possible counts' intervals contain .3. The comparison is conditional on the specified Bernoulli fixture and is not an enterprise endpoint effectiveness result.

## Limitations and next step
Exact binomial inversion requires independent, identically distributed binary device effects and equal interval weights; it is NOT applicable directly to variable interval counts, within-device dependence or shared outage clusters. Next: add an alternative interval for heterogeneous continuous device effects, quantify empirical coverage with increased Monte Carlo trials, and test repository integration. Keep synthetic findings separate from externally validated endpoint evidence.
