## Research question
Does treating devices as independent units produce valid uncertainty intervals when a common outage episode affects many devices simultaneously?

## Model and estimand
Synthetic crossed device-by-episode panel: Y_it = sqrt(rho) U_t + sqrt(1-rho) E_it, where independent standard-normal U_t is a common episode shock and E_it is device-specific noise. There are 100 devices, 10 independent episodes, 1,000 independent Monte Carlo datasets per condition, seed 20261114. Population mean is zero. The naive 95% normal interval uses the between-device variance of device means, divided by the device count.

The exact sampling variance of the grand mean is rho/T + (1-rho)/(N*T); the expected naive device-variance estimate is (1-rho)/(N*T). Thus the naive procedure misses the common-shock contribution rho/T. These formulas are derived from the stated model, not observed endpoint data.

## Executed synthetic results
| rho | Exact grand-mean variance | Expected naive variance | Monte Carlo nominal-95% CI coverage |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 0.001000 | 0.001000 | 94.3% |
| 0.1 | 0.010900 | 0.000900 | 42.8% |
| 0.5 | 0.050500 | 0.000500 | 15.2% |

Monte Carlo standard error is at most 1.58 percentage points (1,000 datasets). All results are synthetic; they are **not** device-trust effectiveness measurements.

## Limitations and next step
The toy design has independent Gaussian episodes and no device-specific persistent random effect, no binary decisions, no missing telemetry, no time autocorrelation and no real endpoints. The naive normal CI is intentionally misspecified under common shocks; the experiment does not yet compare calibrated alternatives. Next: implement and validate episode-level and two-way device/episode inference under crossed dependence, including serially correlated episodes; then connect the validated inference procedure to the longitudinal trust engine. Do not infer real-world effectiveness. No confidential employer data used.
