# Research log — 2026-09-23

## Work completed

Added a frozen-policy transport experiment to test whether a hand-weighted trust policy and an interpretable L2-logistic comparator calibrated only on the nominal synthetic population retain a <=1% empirical unsafe-ALLOW target under previously unseen dependence/overlap conditions. This extends the prior condition-specific learned-baseline experiment without recalibrating to each target distribution.

Fresh literature verification revisited the dataset-shift literature already represented in the project bibliography. Storkey's chapter *When Training and Test Sets Are Different: Characterizing Learning Transfer* in *Dataset Shift in Machine Learning* explicitly characterizes multiple forms of dataset shift and is DOI-registered as `10.7551/mitpress/9780262170055.003.0001`. The broader volume DOI is `10.7551/mitpress/9780262170055.001.0001`. No unverified bibliographic metadata was added.

## Results

Nominal calibration selected hand-policy threshold 0.789 and logistic threshold 0.004426. On an independent nominal synthetic test set, unsafe-ALLOW risk was 0.89% (hand) and 0.91% (logistic). With `rho=0.6` and no added overlap, risk rose to 5.77% and 4.91%. At `rho=0.6, overlap=0.75`, risk reached 31.45% and 30.90%, respectively.

The learned baseline retained greater coverage/recall under shift but failed the frozen safety target similarly. This weakens any claim that better fitted weights alone solve robustness. It supports a narrower claim: deployment-specific calibration must be accompanied by shift monitoring/revalidation, and abstention thresholds should not be represented as invariant security guarantees.

## Evidence status

**Synthetic only.** No confidential employer data or real enterprise endpoint telemetry was used. The shift mechanism is intentionally constructed and does not estimate real-world prevalence or correlation.

## Limitations

- One synthetic generator and one scenario-prevalence vector.
- One family of dependence/overlap shifts.
- Single test seed per condition in the recorded table.
- Logistic threshold is very low because the nominal synthetic classes are highly separable; this itself highlights generator simplicity.
- No claim of production effectiveness or external validity.
- Tests were committed but repository CI execution was not independently observed in this run.

## Next step

Repeat frozen transport over multiple independent seeds and report uncertainty/distribution of unsafe risk, then add a simple shift detector or recalibration trigger to test whether observable feature drift can warn before the unsafe-ALLOW constraint materially fails.
