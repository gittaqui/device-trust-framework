# Research log — 2026-09-22

## Work completed

Added an interpretable learned comparator to test whether failure under class overlap and signal dependence is specific to the hand-selected trust weights. Implemented L2 logistic regression with disjoint synthetic train/calibration/test samples and a matched calibration rule: maximize ordinary-access coverage subject to <=1% empirical unsafe-ALLOW risk. Added targeted utility tests and explicit Python dependencies.

Fresh literature verification added Copas & Corbett (Biometrika 2002), DOI 10.1093/biomet/89.2.315, supporting the methodological requirement not to estimate predictive performance on the same observations used for fitting.

## Results

At rho=0.6 and overlap=0.25, the learned comparator achieved 49.54% test coverage with 1.10% unsafe-ALLOW risk and 83.73% safe-session recall; the calibrated hand policy achieved 17.41% coverage, 0.90% unsafe risk, and 29.48% safe recall. At overlap=0.50 both policies became highly selective. At overlap=0.75 the logistic comparator effectively abstained from ordinary access (0.007% coverage), while the hand policy retained only 0.78% coverage.

These are synthetic results. They show that fixed weights are not optimal for the authored distribution and that severe evidence ambiguity forces a coverage-risk trade-off even for a learned linear comparator. They do not establish real-world superiority of either model.

## Limitations

* Scenario distributions and labels are authored synthetic assumptions.
* The learned model is trained and calibrated separately inside each stress condition, which is favorable and does not test transport under shift.
* One split and one learned model family are reported.
* The 1% calibration risk target is experimental, not a deployment recommendation or guarantee.
* Test risk can exceed the calibration target due to finite-sample variation.
* No confidential employer data were used.

## Next step

Run a prospective transport experiment: train and calibrate both policies only on the nominal synthetic condition, freeze all parameters/thresholds, then evaluate across unseen overlap/dependence shifts and repeated seeds. This directly tests calibration robustness under distribution shift and avoids giving the learned comparator condition-specific recalibration.
