## Analytical result
For the ungated weighted comparator with default weights and independent rectangular scenario ranges, the maximum score is obtained at upper bounds for positive signals and lower bounds for risk signals. Malware maximum = .18(1)+.16(.45)+.16(1)+.12(.90)+.16(1)+.08(1)+.08(.10)+.06(.35)=.789. Protection_missing maximum = .18(.95)+.16(.75)+.16(1)+.12(.85)+.16(.15)+.08(.95)+.08(.80)+.06(.80)=.765. Both exceed the default ALLOW threshold .75, so the gates can prevent some unsafe ALLOW decisions. But the generator assigns all malware and protection_missing rows unsafe and guarantees gate activation in those families. No safe scenario can activate either gate. Consequently, at fixed thresholds the gated model cannot increase safe DENY or decrease safe ALLOW on this generator; this is a structural property, not evidence of real-world gate specificity. The gates cannot affect adversarial_compliant rows because threat risk is at most .88 and coverage at least .80. The current generator therefore cannot measure gate false-positive tradeoffs.

## Next experiment specification
Retain the original generator as a regression benchmark. Add a separate observation-error stress test: preserve scenario labels and latent signals; independently perturb observed threat and coverage with a symmetric, clipped additive error, and separately introduce correlated errors. Use fixed error levels 0, .05, .15, .30 and separate development/evaluation seeds. Compare binary, ungated weighted and gated weighted decisions on identical observed rows. Report safe gate activation, unsafe gate misses, unsafe ALLOW, safe DENY, and STEP_UP by label and scenario. Do not count STEP_UP as containment. Add a simple binary-plus-gates comparator to isolate whether weighted scoring adds value beyond the gates. Match safe ALLOW on development data only and report achieved evaluation rates. This is a proposed synthetic stress test, not a calibrated measurement model.
