## Question
Does the sparse-device percentile-bootstrap undercoverage arise from only 399 bootstrap draws, or is it structural? Added exact enumeration for the ideal infinite-bootstrap-replicate percentile interval.

## Method
For K independent unsafe devices of equal observation length, effects Y_i ~ Bernoulli(p=.3), X~Binomial(K,p). Conditional on X=x, the nonparametric bootstrap has X*~Binomial(K,x/K). Use left-CDF 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of X*/K; sum the known Binomial(K,p) probability of every sample x whose percentile interval covers p.

| K | Exact ideal percentile coverage | Nov 10 finite-bootstrap MC fixed-length coverage | Nov 11 exact Clopper-Pearson coverage |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.420000 | 0.4467 | 1.000000 |
| 5 | 0.801150 | 0.8367 | 0.997570 |
| 20 | 0.947372 | 0.9767 | 0.975218 |
| 100 | 0.950180 | 0.9700 | 0.962549 |

For K=2 only the mixed sample covers p=.3, with probability 2*.3*.7=.42. Thus small-K undercoverage is structural under this restricted synthetic fixture. Finite bootstrap and outer Monte Carlo variability, and empirical quantile conventions, explain differences from Nov 10; do not treat the two procedures as identical.
