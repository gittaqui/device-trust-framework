# Research log — 2026-09-16

## Objective
Extend robustness evaluation beyond the prior scenario-prevalence experiment by testing controlled within-scenario signal shifts while keeping scenario labels, prevalence, model weights, thresholds, and hard gates fixed.

## Fresh literature verification
Verified Sugiyama, Yamada, and du Plessis, *Learning under nonstationarity: covariate shift and class-balance change*, WIREs Computational Statistics 5(6):465–477 (2013), DOI `10.1002/wics.1275`. The paper reviews learning when training/test distributions differ and specifically distinguishes covariate shift from class-balance change. Added the verified citation to `paper/references.bib`.

The already-cited González, Moreo, and Sebastiani (2024), DOI `10.1007/s10618-024-01014-1`, remains directly relevant: robustness to prior-probability shift should not be generalized to other dataset-shift types.

## Work completed
Added `src/evaluate_within_scenario_shift.py`, `tests/test_within_scenario_shift.py`, and `results/within-scenario-shift-results.md`.

The experiment uses 50,000 synthetic sessions per condition with seed `20260916`. It freezes scenario prevalence and labels, then applies pre-specified clipped offsets: identity assurance -0.15; anomaly risk +0.15; posture erosion of -0.10 to compliance, endpoint health, and patch posture; and a mixed adverse shift.

## Results
Baseline additive false ALLOW / safe STEP_UP was 12.87% / 5.19%. Under identity-assurance degradation it became 4.01% / 9.92%; anomaly-risk increase 9.07% / 6.97%; posture erosion 0.56% / 12.76%; mixed adverse 0.99% / 12.29%.

The identity/behavior guard remained below the additive false-ALLOW rate in every condition (baseline 1.42%; shifted conditions 0.31% or lower). The exploratory quorum remained at 0% false ALLOW but imposed additional STEP_UP burden.

## Interpretation
Adverse telemetry/posture shifts make all policies more conservative. Lower false ALLOW therefore cannot be interpreted alone as improved robustness: it is purchased with materially higher verification burden for safe sessions. The result strengthens the manuscript's argument for joint security/friction reporting.

## Limitations
All results are synthetic. Offsets are researcher-selected rather than empirically estimated. Uniform per-scenario distributions, labels, and prevalence remain synthetic constructs. This does not test concept shift, attacker adaptation, production sensor error, or real enterprise endpoint populations. No confidential employer data were used.

The numerical outputs were independently recomputed from the repository's published generator/model definitions before recording. The GitHub connector used for this run does not execute pytest, so no repository-wide test-suite pass is claimed.

## Next step
External validation remains highest priority. If official external telemetry is still unavailable, the next controlled study should perturb signal relationships rather than only marginal levels—for example, introduce correlated telemetry degradation or label-preserving overlap between safe and unsafe scenarios—and quantify whether policy ranking survives that harder shift.
